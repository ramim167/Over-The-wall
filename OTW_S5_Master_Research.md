## 3. The official case brief: "Round 2: Breakthrough, Transform & Lead" `[CASE]`

### 3.1 Marico Bangladesh (case p.1)

- "27 years of empowering change through innovation"; one of the fastest-growing personal care companies in Bangladesh.
- Started in 1999 with **Parachute Coconut Oil**, which became the market leader in branded coconut oil.
- Now multi-category: hair care, male grooming, skincare, hygiene, edible oils, baby care.
- Success built on **consumer-driven innovation** and deep audience understanding.

### 3.2 Marico's three innovation examples (case p.2–7)

These show what Marico considers good innovation.

**1. Parachute Advansed Secrets: "Hair oiling reimagined"**
- **Insight:** the hair-oil category is "often perceived as a product meant for older generations"; young consumers want premium, trend-driven, expressive beauty.
- **Target:** young women (college students, first jobbers, young professionals); "global citizens"; active on Instagram, YouTube and TikTok.
- **Product:** tagline "Stunning Hair, Trending You". Four variants:
  - Almond (damage repair, smooth)
  - Blackseed (long, shiny)
  - Rosemary (voluminous, strong)
  - Onion (hair growth)
- **Marketing:** influencer-led, socially curated campaigns.

**2. Parachute Advansed Extra Care: "Packaging that redefines hair fall care"**
- **Insight:** hair fall is emotionally distressing, especially for women; hair-oil packs highlight ingredients rather than the anxiety, and look and work alike, so there is no reason to switch.
- **Innovation:** **Root Applicator**, which applies oil directly to the scalp for deeper nourishment with no mess.
- **Claim:** "Reduces hair fall in 21 days", based on a study.
- **Pack:** monocarton shows a girl frustrated by hair fall on the upper panel and the solution below.

**3. Parachute Just For Baby (2019): "100% safe, natural nourishing baby care"**
- **Insight:** mothers want proven safety, international certification and expert approval.
- **Credentials:** Made Safe certified, Australian Allergy Tested; olive oil and almond milk.
- **Innovation:** **Baby Soft Touch Bottle**, an ergonomic shape for smooth, even application.

**Takeaway:** Marico explicitly values **packaging innovation** (2 of 3 examples) and a **sharp consumer insight**.

### 5.1 Sales: Mujahidun Nabi, AGM Trade Marketing

**Channel types:**

- **Direct, grocer:**
  - UNG (urban neighbourhood grocer; emergency top-up)
  - UWMG (urban wet-market grocer; bulk, discounts)
  - UGS (urban general store; imported/packaged food)
  - RG (rural grocer; local cluster market, top-up or bulk)
- **Direct, cosmetics and drug:**

### 5.2 Supply Chain: Md. Tanvir Akhtar Tonmoy, Manager Supply Chain Planning

**Concepts:** Plan → Source → Make → Move.

**Product strategy:**
1. Formulation: set a benchmark or identify a competitor
2. Packaging: differentiation in shape, colour or feel
3. Experience: cater to a target market that prefers a specific feel
4. Sustainability: eco-friendly concept

**Planning strategy:**
1. Demand planning (price, sales and demographic positioning of a similar product)
2. On-Time-In-Full supply planning (raw and packaging materials, considering lead times)
3. Network planning (full operational plan, clear stakeholder responsibilities)

**Sourcing strategy, 5 steps:**
1. Local vs Global (costs and lead times)
2. Cost vs Quality (varies by brand and target market)
3. Speed vs Waste (avoid inefficiencies, create agility)
4. Negotiation (number of suppliers in hand)
5. Strategic buying (best time to buy certain raw materials)

**Production strategy:**
- Make-to-Stock vs Make-to-Order (choose by trend or season)
- Capital intensiveness (spend wisely on heavy investment)
- Wastage reduction (recycle waste for packaging)
- Sustainable operation (green concepts for economic benefit and image)

**Servicing strategy:** low-cost logistics; on-time service every time; appropriate channels and transport.

**Sustainability lenses:** Product, Packaging, Operation.

### 5.3 Marketing: Al Jamee Jawad Khan, Category Head

**Winning brand:**
- Customer's view: present in consideration, high loyalty, **inelastic demand**.
- Organisation's view: profitable, grows year on year.

**How brands grow:**
- Increasing **penetration**, or
- Increasing **average consumption**: the same product used more, or **used for multiple purposes**.

**Compelling brand:** Salience, Relevance, Meaningful differentiation.

**Touchpoints:**
- TV, Print, Outdoor, Radio, Activation, Digital.
- The brand task dictates the mix.
- Choose by Impactful / Effective / Efficient.
- Role of each touchpoint: **Unique** vs **Surround**.

**Measure ROI by:** mindshare, change in consideration, increase in imagery, increase in **trial**.

**Benchmarking** (existing practices/codes/behaviours, communication, proposition, benefits): "It will make your big idea sharper & mature."

### 5.4 Finance: Mohammad Rakibul Hasan (most explicit about judging)

**Promise:** turn an idea into an **investable business case**.

| Part | Questions |
|---|---|
| 1 · Commercial logic | Who is the customer? What problem? Why us? |
| 2 · Financial proof | Revenue, contribution, operating profit, cash flow, returns |
| 3 · Executive decision | Investment, risks, decision needed |

Strategy → operating drivers → financial outcomes → cash returns → clear recommendation. "Model the business, not just the spreadsheet."

**Six questions:**
1. What need or problem?
2. How large is the addressable market, and what is the evidence?
3. What drives volume, price, mix and repeat?
4. What incremental costs and capabilities?
5. How much investment and working capital?
6. What return, risk and strategic benefit justify it?

**Judge signal:** "clear problem, credible evidence, explicit value driver."

**Market sizing:** TAM / SAM / SOM. **Value test:** incremental cash inflows − outflows over time vs the required return.

**Drivers:**
- Market = target consumers × purchase frequency × average purchase value.
- Volume = active outlets × units per outlet × selling periods.
- Net revenue = units × company net realisation.
- Example: 10,000 outlets × 20 units/month × 12 = 2.4M units × Tk 28 = **Tk 67.2M**. "Build revenue from operational drivers, not a top-down growth percentage."

**Model sequence:** Inputs → Operating drivers → P&L → Cash flow → Appraisal (NPV, IRR, payback, hurdle) → Scenarios. Keep assumptions separate from formulas.

**Assumptions:**
- Good: "2,000 outlets in Year 1, ramping 500/quarter; pilot productivity 18 units/outlet/month."
- Weak: "10% of the market because our product is better."

**Pitfalls:**
- Over-optimism
- Hidden launch, logistics, promotion or people costs
- **Mixing MRP with net revenue**
- Ignoring **cannibalisation** and capacity

**Controls that impress judges:**
- A source and an owner for each assumption
- An evidence-based base case
- Cross-checking volume, capacity and share
- A downside case with trigger-based mitigation

**Gross-to-net:** Gross sales (units × MRP) − indirect tax (VAT/SD) − trade spending (discounts, offers, rebates, incentives) = **Net revenue**, then − COGS = gross profit, − OPEX (marketing, selling, distribution, overhead) = EBITDA, − D&A, interest, tax = net income.

**Revenue = MRP − indirect tax − sales-related expenditure** (distributor margin, retailer margin, sales-force incentives, customer discounts and offers).

**Trade value chain example:**

| Line | Tk |
|---|---|
| MRP | 50.00 |
| Indirect tax | −7.50 |
| Retailer commission | −3.00 |
| Distributor commission | −2.00 |
| Company trade spend | −2.50 |
| **Net realisation** | **35.00** |

**Pricing approaches:** cost-plus, competitive, skimming, penetration, value-based. The price must work for the consumer, the channel and the company's contribution.

**COGS:** direct (raw and packaging materials, labour, direct manufacturing) + indirect (utilities, factory overhead).

**Worked P&L:** 1M units × MRP Tk 50 = Tk 50M gross; −15M = Tk 35M net; COGS 18.2 → GP 16.8 (**48%**); OPEX 10.5 → EBITDA 6.3 (**18%**); dep 0.8, interest 0.2, tax 1.3 → NI 4.0. "**Judges check the denominator: use net revenue, not MRP value.**"

**Align P&L with operations:** marketing spend matches campaign timing and conversion; salesforce matches outlet expansion; capacity, yield and wastage support the forecast; separate one-off from recurring costs, but include both. **Judge signal:** "every material P&L line has a business driver and a clear owner."

**Profit ≠ cash:** example EBITDA +6.3, tax −1.0, NWC −4.0, CAPEX −5.0 → **FCF −3.7**. **Judge signal:** "the team understands cash timing and can explain the **peak funding requirement**."

**Appraisal:**
- NPV is the primary measure.
- IRR vs hurdle rate (it can mislead).
- Payback, simple and discounted. Example: 10 ÷ 2.5 = 4 years.
- IRR example cash flows: −10, +3, +4, +5, +3.

**Promotion economics:** break-even incremental units = promo cost ÷ contribution per unit (e.g., 500,000 ÷ 25 = 20,000). Measure uplift, cannibalisation, forward buying and post-promo drop.

**Scenario table:**

| Driver | Worst | Base | Best |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | −20% | Plan | +15% |
| Net realisation | −5% | Plan | +2% |
| COGS | +8% | Plan | −3% |
| Launch delay | 3 months | On time | On time |
| Distribution ramp | Slow | Expected | Faster |

Report net revenue, EBITDA, peak cash, NPV/IRR and the decision threshold.

**Tough-question method:** answer in the order **conclusion → number → evidence → action**.
- "20% lower sales?"
- "Why this price?"
- "Biggest assumption?" (name it, show evidence and sensitivity, propose a pilot)
- "Why not invest elsewhere?"
- "How will you know it is working?" (leading indicators, milestones, owner, stop/go)

### 5.5 Manufacturing: Quazi Azizul Hassan, Operations

- **Core idea:** "A great idea is only the beginning". **Desirable ≠ manufacturable.** Idea → working prototype → commercial product (repeatable, scalable, economical). "The gap is not invention. It is manufacturing readiness."
- **Five questions:**
  1. Can we make it? (feasibility)
  2. Enough? (capacity)
  3. Consistently? (quality)
  4. Profitably? (economics)
  5. Responsibly? (responsibility)
- **Manufacturing brief:** Product, Pack, Volume, Market. The business defines WHAT; manufacturing determines HOW.
- **Feasibility funnel:** Idea → Evaluate → Challenge → Refine → Decide.
- **8-lens check:** Product & Process, Material, Packaging, Capacity, Cost, Quality & Compliance, Supply Chain, Timeline. "One weak lens can change the manufacturing route."
- **MVC, cost and margin:** technical feasibility ≠ commercial viability. Missed costs: **MOQ, capacity, space cost**.
- **Route:** **Make** (existing capability) / **Buy** (outsource) / **Modify** (adapt capability) / **Redesign** (change the proposition). "Feasible does not automatically mean make."
- **FACTORY readiness:** Feasibility, Affordability, Capability, Time, Operation, Risk, Yield & scalability. Leads to a go / resolve / redesign decision.

## 10. Why WaterSmart was chosen

1. **Avoids the crowd, keeps a nearby worry.** Most teams will pitch hair-fall oils, serums or applicators. WaterSmart asks whether rough, weak or breaking hair is partly a **water** problem: "দোষ কি চুলের, নাকি পানির?" It does **not** claim water causes hair fall.
2. **Exact case wording.** Hair Cleansing: "emerging consumer needs and **usage occasions**" (moving to a hostel or new city, migrant life, coastal living). Product **and** packaging innovation.
3. **Lab-testable claims.** Mineral build-up removal and lather in hard or saline water can be measured. They are **proposed** claims until Marico R&D tests a formula; the breakage claim needs a further study.
4. **Proven abroad, under-served here.** Dove Hard Water Defense (Gulf); US search trend. In Bangladesh, imported hard-water shampoos appear on cross-border and e-commerce listings at premium prices; our desk search found **no affordable local option** `[VALIDATE: shelf audit]`.
5. **Marico can make it.** Naturale brand + coconut milk protein + existing shampoo lines (Modify) + sachet GT reach. Export only after each market's registration.
6. **Three pivots + impact.** Innovation, Diversification, Scaling Up; coastal salinity climate adaptation.
7. **Memorable demo.** TDS meter, hardness strip and iron strip on hall water; a strip changing colour; a same-shampoo foam test ("same shampoo, different water"); "we tested ▢ water sources".

*(v1 tagline "দোষ চুলের না, পানির।" asserted causation; replaced.)*

**One-liner:** A proposed chelating Naturale shampoo first for adult Movers using iron/hard-water sources: Iron & Hard Water first, with LonaJol as a conditional Phase 2, a **proposed PaniCheck** hardness strip in the 340ml launch cap, subject to a viable landed quote, and a **Pani Map** that shows dated regional groundwater information where source coverage is available and prompts a home test.

### 11.1 Insight (three layers, honestly weighted)

1. **Consumer belief** `[VALIDATE planned n=25]`: "Since I moved, my hair feels different." Ask about hair change **before** mentioning water; record whether water is mentioned unprompted.
2. **Science (mixed, limited):**
   - Luqman 2018 (PMID 30034190): matched hair segments from 70 men; tensile strength untreated 255.49, deionized 254.84, hard water 234.16 (P=0.001).
   - Luqman 2016 (PMID 27654734): hard water weakened hair; not a hair-loss finding.
   - Srinivasan 2013 (PMID 24574692): no significant difference between hard and deionized water.

3. **Bangladesh water:** BGS/DPHE National Hydrochemical Survey (3,534 boreholes, 61 districts, 1998–1999 sampling; 2001 report); Naser 2018 (654 wells, median iron 0.91 mg/L, IQR 0.36–2.01); coastal salinity (~29% coastal population; UNDP: 73% in 5 upazilas drink saline water; Mongla women's verbatim).

#### Launch focus: Movers [HYPOTHESIS]

Start with adult hall/mess students and young workers who moved to a new city or changed their bathing-water source. The proposed problem is rough/sticky feel, mineral residue or disappointing lather; stains are a cue, not proof of a hair-damage cause. Select a launch area using real interviews, current source tests and outlet reach.

**Phase 1:** Iron & Hard Water, 340ml + proposed PaniCheck, 180ml and 6ml sachet. **Phase 2:** LonaJol only after its separate formula, safety, stability, regulatory and coastal-use gates.
