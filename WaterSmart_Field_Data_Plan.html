Method limits to say out loud. A TDS meter estimates dissolved solids from conductivity: it cannot tell iron from salt or calcium. Hardness strips read mainly calcium and magnesium (iron and copper can interfere). Iron needs its own strip. Bottled water is a low-mineral reference, not proof of "soft". Foam height is not cleaning or hair benefit, and a same-shampoo foam test shows only that experiment's lather differences, not WaterSmart's advantage. 1–3★ reviews are a biased sample: report themes, not prevalence.

A3 · Foam test

Protocol: identical clear jars with 200 ml of water each, same temperature: (1) bottled (low-mineral reference), (2) hall or home tap, (3) measured NaCl 5 g made up to 1 litre final solution, (4) measured NaCl 25 g made up to 1 litre as a stress test only. Take 200 ml from each. These defined solutions are not proof of local coastal concentrations; do not estimate mass by teaspoon. Add 1 ml of the same shampoo, cover, shake 10 times, wait 30 s, measure foam height with a ruler. Repeat each water 3 times and report the average. Fixed camera, all jars in one frame.
What this proves and what it doesn't. Report the measured lather differences, including a null or opposite result. It does not establish a universal effect of water. It does not show cleaning, hair benefit, or that WaterSmart works better: that needs a prototype formula from Marico R&D. Caption it in the video as "same shampoo, different water".
